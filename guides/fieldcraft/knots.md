# Eight knots that do almost everything

Learn these with a real rope in your hands, slowly, then with your eyes closed. A knot you have to look up is a knot you do not know. The Knots book in the library on this drive shows each one in pictures and animation: search for the name of the knot.

The working end is the short end you tie with. The standing part is the rest of the rope.

## 1. Square knot: tie two ends together

For bandages and bundles. Not for weight: it can slip.

1. Right over left and under.
2. Left over right and under.
3. Both ends come out on the same side, beside their own standing part. If they come out on opposite sides, it is a granny knot and will slip.

## 2. Bowline: a loop that will not slip or jam

For a rescue loop around a person, tying a line to a ring or a tree, or a loop at the end of a rope.

1. Make a small overhand loop in the standing part, the working end on top.
2. Bring the working end up through the loop from below: the rabbit comes out of the hole.
3. Pass it behind the standing part: around the tree.
4. Back down through the small loop: back into the hole.
5. Hold the working end and the side of the big loop together and pull the standing part tight.

Leave a tail at least as long as your hand.

## 3. Clove hitch: tie onto a pole, fast

For starting lashings and tying to a post. It can slip under a changing pull, so finish it with half hitches if it matters.

1. Wrap the rope around the pole.
2. Cross over itself and wrap around again.
3. Tuck the working end under that second wrap, so it comes out between the two wraps.

## 4. Two half hitches: tie to a ring, a post or a tree

1. Pass the rope around the post.
2. Bring the working end around the standing part and through the loop it makes: one half hitch.
3. Do it again, the same way round: two half hitches. Snug them together.

## 5. Taut line hitch: a line you can tighten and loosen

For tent and tarp lines. Slide the knot to make the line tight; it holds where you leave it.

1. Pass the rope around the stake, back toward where it came from.
2. Wrap the working end twice around the standing part, inside the loop, working toward the stake.
3. Then once more around the standing part, outside the loop, beyond the first two wraps.
4. Tuck it under itself and pull snug. Slide the knot to tension the line.

## 6. Trucker's hitch: pull a load down tight

For tying down a load, or a ridge line drum tight. It gives you a pulley's worth of pull.

1. Tie the rope at one end.
2. Partway along, make a fixed loop in the standing part (a slipped overhand, or a figure eight on a bight).
3. Pass the working end around the anchor at the other side, and back up through that loop.
4. Pull down hard on the working end. The loop acts like a pulley.
5. Lock it with two half hitches below the loop.

## 7. Sheet bend: join two ropes of different sizes

1. Bend the thicker rope into a U.
2. Pass the thinner end up through the U from below.
3. Take it around behind both legs of the U.
4. Tuck it under itself, across the front of the U, not through it.
5. Both short ends should end up on the same side. Pull tight.

## 8. Figure eight: a stopper, and a strong loop

* **Stopper:** make a loop, pass the working end behind the standing part, then down through the loop. It looks like an 8. It keeps a rope from pulling through a hole or a pulley.
* **Figure eight loop:** double the end of the rope and tie the same knot with the doubled part. A strong loop that is easy to check: it should look like two neat 8s side by side.

## Rope sense

* Coil it neatly and keep it dry. Wet, dirty and sunlit rope weakens.
* Never trust a rope for climbing or holding a person unless it is made for it.
* Cut nylon rope by melting the end so it does not unravel. Tape or whip natural fiber rope.
