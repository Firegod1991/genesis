# Radio card: who to call, on what, and how

When the phones are down, radio still works. Print this and tape it inside the radio case. The Radio page of Genesis has the full list of frequencies, and the radio programs are on the drive.

## Listen first

* **NOAA Weather Radio,** United States: 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525 and 162.550 MHz. Warnings and news from the National Weather Service, around the clock. Any weather radio, scanner or many handheld radios receive it.
* **AM and FM broadcast:** the local station named in your area's emergency plan carries official instructions.
* Listening is legal almost everywhere. Transmitting is where the rules are.

## Channels people use

| Radio | Channel | Frequency | What for |
|---|---|---|---|
| FRS or GMRS walkie talkie | 1 | 462.5625 MHz | Common first channel for family and neighbors |
| FRS or GMRS | 3 | 462.6125 MHz | Many preparedness groups listen here |
| GMRS | 20, tone 141.3 | 462.675 MHz | Travelers and emergency assistance |
| CB | 9 | 27.065 MHz | Emergency and help on the road |
| CB | 19 | 27.185 MHz | Truckers: road and traffic news |
| Marine VHF | 16 | 156.800 MHz | Distress and calling on the water; the Coast Guard listens |
| Ham, 2 meters | simplex | 146.520 MHz | National calling frequency |
| Ham, 70 centimeters | simplex | 446.000 MHz | National calling frequency |

Agree with your family on one channel and a time to listen, and write it in the family plan.

## Licenses in the United States

* **FRS:** no license. Low power walkie talkies.
* **GMRS:** a license from the FCC, no test, one fee covers the whole family. Lets you use more power and repeaters.
* **CB:** no license.
* **Ham (amateur radio):** a license with a short exam. The widest reach of all: across town on a handheld, across the world on the higher bands.
* **Marine VHF:** for boats.

When a life or property is in immediate danger and no normal way of calling for help works, the amateur rules allow any means of communication. Outside a real emergency, follow the rules for each radio.

## How to call for help

### Life in danger: MAYDAY

Say it slowly and clearly, then let go of the button and listen:

1. **"MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY."**
2. **"This is"** your name or call sign, three times.
3. **"MAYDAY,"** your name once more.
4. **Where you are.** As exact as you can: an address, a road and mile marker, a landmark, or a position from a map or GPS.
5. **What is wrong.** "Man down, bleeding badly." "House fire." "Boat sinking."
6. **How many people,** and how many are hurt.
7. **What help you need.**
8. **"Over."**

If no one answers, wait a minute and repeat. Try other channels.

### Urgent but not life or death: PAN PAN

Same pattern, starting **"PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN."** A broken down boat drifting, a lost person who is safe for now.

### With a light or a whistle

**SOS:** three short, three long, three short. **... --- ...** Repeat with a pause.

## Make it reach farther

* **Height beats power.** Climb a hill, a roof, the top floor. A handheld on a ridge reaches farther than a big radio in a valley.
* **Hold the radio upright,** antenna straight up, away from your body and metal.
* **Short calls, long listens.** Batteries last longer when you listen more than you talk.
* **Keep batteries warm** in a pocket in cold weather. Carry spares, and a way to charge them.

## Meshtastic: text with no phone network

Small, cheap radios that pair with a phone over Bluetooth and pass text messages from radio to radio across miles, with no towers and no license.

1. Install the Meshtastic app. It is in the phone apps on this drive.
2. Pair the radio with the phone.
3. Set the **region** first. In the United States it is **US.** Nothing works until the region is set.
4. Radios on the same channel and settings talk to each other. Each one passes messages along, so more radios means more reach.

## Practice

Turn on the radios once a month. Call each other on the family channel. Check the batteries. A radio nobody has used is a radio nobody can use.
