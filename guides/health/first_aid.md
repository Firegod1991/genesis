# First aid: stop the bleeding and keep them alive

What to do in the first minutes, in the order it matters. This card does not replace training. Take a Stop the Bleed class and a CPR class: both are short, often free, and teach with your hands what no page can. The medical books on this drive go deeper once the person is stable.

## First: you, then them

* **Make sure it is safe for you.** Traffic, fire, falling things, live wires, the person who did it. A second casualty helps no one.
* **Call for help now,** or send someone to. 911 in the United States, 112 in Europe and from most mobile phones. No signal? See the radio card on the Disaster shelf.
* **Gloves** if you have them. A plastic bag over the hand if you do not.

Then look for the thing that kills fastest. In this order:

1. **Bleeding** that spurts, pools, or soaks through clothing.
2. **Breathing.** Are they breathing normally?
3. **Everything else.**

## Bleeding

Blood loss can kill in three to five minutes. Act before anything else.

### Press

Put both hands on the wound, one over the other, and push hard, straight down. Use a cloth if you have one, but do not wait for one. Keep pressing. Do not lift to look.

### Pack a deep wound

A wound you cannot press flat, in the armpit, the groin, the neck or deep in a limb:

1. Push gauze or clean cloth right into the wound, onto the bleeding, with your fingers.
2. Keep stuffing it in until the wound is full and the packing stands above the skin.
3. Press down on it with both hands and hold, hard, for at least three minutes. Longer with plain cloth.

Never put a tourniquet on the neck, and never pack a wound in the chest or the belly: just press and cover.

### Tourniquet: for an arm or a leg that will not stop

1. Put it **high and tight** on the limb, a hand's width above the wound, never on a joint. If you cannot see the wound clearly, go as high on the limb as you can.
2. Pull the strap tight, then twist the rod until the **bleeding stops.** It will hurt. That is right.
3. Lock the rod. **Write the time** on the tourniquet or on the skin.
4. Do not loosen it. Only a medic or a doctor takes it off.

A real tourniquet, one made for the job, works far better than a belt. Keep one in the car and the go bag. If you must improvise, use a strip of cloth at least two inches wide and a stick to twist it, never a cord or wire.

## Not breathing: CPR

If they do not respond and are not breathing, or only gasp:

1. Call for help and send someone for an **AED,** the defibrillator in a box on the wall of many public buildings.
2. Put them on their back on something firm.
3. Heel of one hand on the center of the chest, the other hand on top. Arms straight.
4. Push **hard and fast:** at least two inches deep, 100 to 120 times a minute. That is the beat of the song "Stayin' Alive." Let the chest come all the way back up between pushes.
5. Trained in rescue breaths? Give 2 breaths after every 30 pushes. Not trained, or unwilling? Just push. Pushing alone still saves lives.
6. When the AED comes, switch it on and do what it says. Keep pushing until it tells you to stop or help takes over.

For a baby, use two fingers in the center of the chest, about one and a half inches deep.

## Choking

If they can cough or speak, let them cough. If they cannot breathe, speak or cough:

* **Adult or child:** stand behind them. Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. Then 5 **abdominal thrusts:** a fist just above the navel, your other hand over it, sharp pulls in and up. Repeat until it comes out or they collapse. If they collapse, start CPR.
* **Baby:** face down along your forearm, head lower than the body. 5 back blows, then turn over and 5 chest pushes with two fingers. Repeat.

## Shock

Pale, cold and clammy skin, fast weak pulse, fast breathing, confusion, thirst. It follows bad bleeding, burns and serious injury, and it kills.

* Stop any bleeding first.
* Lay them flat. Keep them warm: something under them and something over them.
* Nothing to eat or drink.
* Talk to them. Stay with them. Get help.

## Burns

1. Cool the burn under cool running water for **20 minutes.** Not ice, not butter, not ointment.
2. Take off rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells. Do not pull off cloth that is stuck.
3. Cover loosely with clean plastic wrap or a clean, dry cloth.
4. Keep the rest of them warm. A large burn can make a person dangerously cold.

Get help for any burn larger than their palm, and for any burn on the face, hands, feet, groin or across a joint, or from chemicals or electricity.

## Broken bones and sprains

* Do not try to straighten it. **Splint it as you find it,** with something rigid (a board, a rolled magazine, a stick) padded and tied on so it holds the joint above and the joint below the break.
* Check that fingers or toes beyond the splint stay warm, pink and can feel. If not, loosen it.
* A bone through the skin: cover the wound, do not push the bone back.
* **Neck or back:** if they fell from height, were in a crash, or have neck pain, numbness or weakness, do not move them unless they are in danger where they lie. Keep the head still.

## Too cold

Shivering, clumsy, mumbling, confused. Then shivering stops, which is worse.

* Get them out of the wind and wet. Replace wet clothes with dry.
* Insulate from the ground. Wrap them, head included. Share body heat in a sleeping bag.
* Warm sweet drinks only if they are awake and can swallow.
* Handle a very cold person gently: rough handling can stop the heart.

## Too hot

* **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweat, cramps, faint, headache. Shade, lie down, loosen clothes, sip water, cool them with wet cloths.
* **Heat stroke:** hot skin, confused, collapses, may stop sweating. This kills. **Cool them now,** as fast as you can: cold water over the whole body, or sit them in cold water, ice packs in the neck, armpits and groin, fanning. Get help.

## What to keep in the kit

* A tourniquet made for the job, and know how to use it
* Gauze for packing, and pressure bandages
* Gloves, tape, shears
* A rescue blanket
* Pain relief and any medicine your family needs, with spares

Then take the class.
